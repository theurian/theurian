# 2026-08-30 — #199 unit A: auditing the threat model's claims against `src/`

Anchor: `main 06de58a`, tree clean. Every count below is the output of a command,
re-run against that commit while writing this log; the commands are listed at the
end. The corrections themselves are in
[PR #425](https://github.com/theurian/theurian/pull/425).

#199 exists because three separate defects turned out to be one shape: a
threat-model sentence describing a control that `src/` does not implement, or
implements differently. #129, T-15 and T-6 each arrived as its own bug. This unit
audited the whole document rather than the next symptom.

## What the audit is a population of

An audit is only as good as its key, and a key is a claim: *these are all the
places a control claim can hide.* The key went wrong twice before it held, and
both regressions have the same cause, which is the finding this log exists to
record.

### The three keys, v3

| Key | What it selects | Members at `06de58a` |
| :-- | :-- | --: |
| **Container census** | every bold-labelled block opener, at any blockquote nesting, classified by whether its body names a `src` symbol | 276 openers · 88 already inside another key · 188 outside · 15 needing triage |
| **1e retraction blocks** | structural: a marker is a member iff it *opens* a retraction block not already inside one | 21 members (16 corrections + 5 discharges), 7 continuations |
| **Verb sweep** | active-voice control verbs anywhere in the document, each hit assigned to its owning block | 134 hits · 32 inside a keyed member · 102 triaged outside |

The census is load-bearing and the verb sweep is the cross-check — which is the
opposite of how the residual was framed going in. `:4940`, labelled **"Control,
part A — read-side, serve-time"**, carries **zero** verb-sweep hits: its claim is
spelled "reads each … referenced id", and no listed verb appears. The census
finds it; the sweep does not. Two keys covering each other's blind spots is the
design, not redundancy.

### Key history, and why each version regressed

| Version | Key for control claims | What it missed, and why |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **v1** | `line.startswith("**Controls")` | A prefix match on one plural noun. Missed the three `*Future controls, not shipped:*` paragraphs and every `> **Corrected …` blockquote — 2 of the 4 corrections found live outside all of v1's keys. |
| **v2** | v1 + a `1d` key for the Future-controls paragraphs + a regex `1e` key for retraction markers | The regex could not settle 1e: `"What it said."` is a *continuation* at `:1717` and a block *opener* at `:4258` — the same string, two roles. v2's first count of 17 over-counted by 3 continuations and under-counted by 7 top-level markers. |
| **v3** | container census (openers, any nesting, classified) + structural 1e + verb sweep as cross-check | See the closure statement below. |

**Each version stopped at a boundary its own author expected.** v1's author
expected a control claim to be labelled `**Controls`, so two of them labelled
`**Control` — singular — were invisible, missed by one character. v2's author
expected a marker's text to determine its role, so a string that is an opener in
one place and a continuation in another could not be counted. Neither key was
careless; each encoded an assumption its author did not know was an assumption.
That is why a stated key matters more than a careful reader: a reader cannot
attack an assumption nobody wrote down.

## The closure statement

**v3 is the terminal *achievable* form, not a proof.** A claim that is neither a
bold-labelled block opener nor verb-bearing escapes both keys. That escape space
is characterized rather than assumed away, and at `06de58a` it is **measured
empty of control-capability claims** — the two keys' union covered everything the
census surfaced *in that class* — so any future finding there is a named v4, not
an open-ended regress. What that buys is a bounded argument: the next reader
attacks the escape space or the measurement, both of which are written down,
instead of re-deriving the population.

**The class qualifier is load-bearing, and the v4 it reserves arrived before this
PR merged.** The keys measure claims about what the codebase *can do*. An
**owner-cite** — an issue named as owning a residual or a future control — is a
claim about the *tracker*, not about a capability, and it is a distinct
population outside both keys. `threat-model.md:3820` reads "[#15] removes this
face and T-17a's collection statistics together", and #15 is closed; the residual
is live. Found by a parallel lane after this audit closed, and filed as
[#427](https://github.com/theurian/theurian/issues/427), which sweeps the
owner-cite population on its own stated key and classifies each cite as owner or
history. C-2 corrected three owner-cites because the *entries* they sat in were
keyed members; nothing keyed the owner-cite shape itself, so the audit found
those three by where they lived rather than by what they claimed. **The
control-claim completeness argument above stands as measured. What it never
covered is now named.**

### Known limitations of these keys

**A false positive in the 1e key**, found while writing this log: a bold run that
begins a line by accident of *wrapping* is indistinguishable from a block opener.
A sentence in this PR's own T-16 correction wrapped so that `**closed**` started
a line, and the key counted a 22nd discharge block that does not exist. Rewrapped
rather than special-cased — the key is right that a line-opening bold run is how
this document opens a block, and prose that wraps that way is ambiguous to a
human reader too.

**A false negative across all three keys: the owner-cite.** The example is
`threat-model.md:3820`, a continuation line inside a correction blockquote — its
leading `>` markers elided here, because a fenced block whose lines open with `>`
is the one thing `test_flattening_blockquotes_does_not_touch_fenced_content`
exists to forbid, and this log is not worth making `_flatten_blockquotes`
fence-aware for:

```
[#15](https://github.com/theurian/theurian/issues/15) removes this face and
T-17a's collection statistics together, which is the evidence that they are one
class rather than two findings.
```

#15 is `CLOSED COMPLETED` (2026-08-10) and the residual is live, so the sentence
names a closed issue as the owner of an open face — C-2's class, at a site C-2
never reached. Each key misses it for its own reason, and each was measured:

| Key | Why it misses | Check |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Container census | the line is not a bold-labelled block opener — it opens with `[`, not `**` | `grep -cE '^[ \t>]*\*\*[^*]'` on the line → `0` |
| Verb sweep | "removes" is not in the verb alternation, which lists control verbs (`refuses`, `rejects`, `bounds`, …) — an owner-cite uses the vocabulary of *fixing*, not of *enforcing* | the verb regex against the line → no match |
| Structural 1e | **not blind — it reached the text and looked at something else.** The enclosing member opens at `:3701` and was dispositioned on its retraction premise (`Visibility.cleared` has exactly two production callers, verified). Owner liveness was never the axis. | the 1e member list: the last member before `:3820` is `:3701` |

The third row is the instructive one. A key can *cover* a line and still miss the
claim on it, because coverage is about text and a disposition is about a
question. "Which claims did the key reach" and "which questions did the audit ask
of them" are two different completeness arguments, and only the first was written
down here. Owned by [#427](https://github.com/theurian/theurian/issues/427).

## The meta-finding

**Four enumeration misses in this arc share one root cause: a key chosen by what
its author expected to find.**

1. v1's `**Controls` prefix missed `**Control, part A/B`, by one character.
2. v1's key reached no `*Future controls, not shipped:*` paragraph, where C-1 lives.
3. v1's key reached no `> **Corrected …` blockquote, where C-3 lives — the
   highest-risk text in the document, since a correction block is written to
   replace a falsified premise and then never re-derived.
4. The verb sweep, proposed as the primary key, would have missed `:4940` outright.

The defense is not a more careful reader. It is **a mechanical key run to
exhaustion, plus a stated key that a second reader can attack.** The
census-plus-sweep pair is that defense by construction: the census is mechanical
and total over containers, the sweep is orthogonal to it, and both are written
down here in a form that can be re-run and disagreed with.

The corollary the corrections themselves demonstrate: **a premise that has to
stay true belongs in a test that reddens, not in a sentence.** C-1's conclusion
survives because
`test_the_shipped_modules_that_name_a_watched_config_key_are_the_recorded_ones`
pins the reader set as an equality over the whole source tree. C-3's premise had
no such pin, which is why a correction block written to fix a wrongly-named
mechanism went stale the same way within one milestone.

## M-1 — YAML alias expansion cost in `projection._walk`

Owed by #199's body: `MAX_DEPTH` bounds depth, and breadth via YAML aliases was
recorded as unmeasured, with the guess that `_StrictLoader`'s node cache bounds
it.

**Measured answer: the loader's node cache does not bound the walk.** `_walk`
carries no memo — unlike `_external_refs`'s `descended` — so a node reached by
*n* paths is entered *n* times. What bounds it is `MAX_PROJECTION_CHARS`, charged
in `_Spend.emit` *during* the walk; not `MAX_PROJECTION_NODES`, and not the
parse.

### The first measurement was wrong three ways, and the round found all three

**The figures this section originally carried are withdrawn, not adjusted.** They
were reported to the threat model as an ingestion-path cost and were none of the
things that phrase means. Recorded here in full because the *shape* of the error
is more reusable than the number:

| What was measured | What ingestion actually does | Effect on the figure |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `project_checked`, which **raises** on a budget | `project`, which **truncates** and indexes the result (`ingestion_service.py:225`) | measured a function with no production caller at all |
| wall clock **under `tracemalloc`** | — | inflated 6.4–7.6× |
| the projection **alone** | parse **then** project | omitted the term that dominates (94% at the gate's ceiling) |

Compounding: a ~3.1 s "ingestion-path" figure against a real clean end-to-end
cost of 2.27 s on that document — and the real *worst* case, on the widest
document the gate admits, is 6.32 s, which the original never looked at because
it had stopped at the document the *projection* was slowest on.

The withdrawn comparison is the sharpest lesson. It said the alias shape cost
~6.5× the 0.48 s `projection.py::MAX_PROJECTION_NODES` records. That put an
instrumented number against a clean one:

```
amendment said: worst alias 3.14 s is ~6.5x the node-ceiling 0.48 s
clean/clean   : 0.379 s vs 0.397 s  ->  0.95x
traced/traced : 2.844 s vs 2.976 s  ->  0.96x
traced/clean (what the amendment did): 2.844 s vs 0.397 s -> 7.16x
```

Clean on clean the two shapes are indistinguishable, so the comparison is not
restated in a corrected form — **it never had a finding in it.** A ratio between
two numbers taken on different instruments is not a weak measurement; it is not a
measurement.

### Re-measured: the shipped path

`YamlParser.parse` → `project`. Wall clock **clean**, never in a `tracemalloc`
pass; memory reported as process RSS delta, with python-heap peak taken
separately and labelled. CPython 3.12.2, macOS arm64, at branch commit `b0f1ee9`
(`projection.py`, `yaml_loading.py` and `ingestion_service.py` identical to
`main`).

```
=== CLEAN wall clock (no tracemalloc), shipped path: YamlParser.parse -> project ===
worst shape FOUND (1.29 MB)   1,289,309 B | parse 1.886s | project 0.384s | TOTAL 2.270s | parse share 83.1% | RSS + 80.6 MB | TRUNCATED+indexed (2,097,127 chars)
widest the 4 MiB gate admits  4,194,295 B | parse 5.941s | project 0.375s | TOTAL 6.316s | parse share 94.1% | RSS +157.7 MB | TRUNCATED+indexed (2,097,127 chars)

=== second pass, python-heap peak under tracemalloc (timings here are inflated) ===
widest the 4 MiB gate admits  4,194,295 B | parse 28.981s | project 3.212s | TOTAL 32.193s | RSS +258.0 MB | heap-peak 226.2 MB | TRUNCATED+indexed
```

Conclusions, each carrying its number:

1. **The structural finding survives the re-measurement.** `_walk` carries no
   memo, so a node reached by *n* paths is entered *n* times: at 22 alias levels
   the parsed graph holds **48** objects while the walk enters **120,491**. Visit
   counts are instrument-independent, which is why this half stood while every
   timing beside it fell.
2. **The projection is not where the money goes.** `MAX_PROJECTION_CHARS` does
   bind the walk rather than `MAX_PROJECTION_NODES`, but the walk is ~6% of
   end-to-end cost at the gate's ceiling. **`MAX_YAML_BYTES` is the bound that
   matters for T-6's ingestion cost**, and the original amendment never named it.
3. **The shipped path admits the document.** `project` truncates at
   `MAX_PROJECTION_CHARS`, appends `SIZE_MARKER` and returns the text to be
   indexed. Nothing refuses. "Refused" appeared throughout the first measurement
   because `project_checked` refuses — and nothing calls it.
4. **Residual, MEDIUM:** ~6.3 s single-threaded CPU-bound work and ~158 MB RSS
   for one clone-reachable document, after which a truncated projection is
   indexed. Availability, not disclosure; ingestion-side, so #26 (query-side)
   does not own it. **This is the worst shape *found*, not an established
   maximum** — no exhaustive search over document shapes was run. T-6's recorded
   decision that no ingestion-side timeout is filed is unchanged.
